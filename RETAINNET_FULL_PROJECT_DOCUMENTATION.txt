1. INTRODUCTION & PROBLEM STATEMENT
====================================================================================================

1.1 LIMITATIONS OF BINARY CHURN CLASSIFICATION
Most standard churn projects treat customer retention as a binary classification problem. An arbitrary cutoff window (for example, 30 or 90 days) is chosen, and each customer is labeled either y = 1 (churned) or y = 0 (active). While straightforward to implement, that setup breaks down on subscription datasets for three practical reasons:

1. Incomplete Observation of Active Customers (Right-Censoring):
   When a dataset snapshot is taken, active customers have not churned yet. All we actually know is that their true lifetime T* exceeds their current observed tenure C. Labeling a 2-month active subscriber and a 50-month active subscriber both as y = 0 throws away tenure information and biases probability estimates downward.

2. No Time-to-Event Trajectory:
   A binary classifier outputs a single score without indicating when the subscriber is likely to leave. From a retention budget perspective, a customer whose survival probability drops steeply in the next 3 months requires a different intervention than one whose risk gradually increases after month 24.

3. Static Customer Lifetime Value (CLTV) Assumptions:
   Textbook CLTV formulas such as CLTV = ARPU * [r / (1 + d - r)] - CAC assume a constant monthly retention rate r across the entire subscriber base. In real telecom and SaaS cohorts, hazard rates vary substantially by contract type, monthly bill, support ticket volume, and tenure.

1.2 FORMULATING RETENTION AS RIGHT-CENSORED TIME-TO-EVENT MODELING
RetainNet models each subscriber record as a right-censored survival pair (T_i, E_i):
- T_i = min(T_i*, C_i) is the observed duration in months (either the month the subscriber churned or their current active tenure at the end of the observation window).
- E_i in {0, 1} is the event indicator, where E_i = 1 denotes an observed churn event and E_i = 0 denotes a right-censored active subscriber.

Given a subscriber's covariate vector x_i, the models in RetainNet estimate an Individualized Survival Distribution (ISD):
    S(t | x_i) = P(T > t | x_i),   for t in [1, H]
and pass that trajectory into a numerical quadrature engine (`DynamicLTVCalculator`) to compute discounted lifetime value:
    CLTV(x_i) = \int_0^H S(t | x_i) * ARPU_i * (1 + d)^(-t) dt - CAC

1.3 SUMMARY OF IMPLEMENTED COMPONENTS
- A 3-stage training pipeline (`RetainNetHybridPipeline`) combining K-Means behavioral clustering, training-set propensity boundary filtering, and survival regression.
- Four survival models with a consistent `fit`, `predict_risk`, and `predict_survival_function` API: PyTorch `DeepSurvModel`, PyTorch `DeepHitModel`, `CoxPHBaselineModel`, and `RandomSurvivalForestBaselineModel`.
- A universal CSV validation and adaptation layer (`retainnet/data/validator.py` and `dashboard.py`) that ingests Telco, Retail Sales, E-Commerce, and SaaS CSV files, strips currency formatting, blocks formula injection, and supports both labeled cohorts and inference-only cohorts.
- Censoring-aware conformal prediction intervals (`ConformalSurvivalCalibrator`), population drift metrics (`DriftDetector`), and cluster-level baseline survival priors (`AmortizedSurvivalInference`).
- A self-contained six-tab Streamlit application (`dashboard.py`) and a command-line runner (`main.py`) backed by 119 automated unit and integration tests.

2. MATHEMATICAL FORMULATIONS & METHODOLOGY
====================================================================================================

2.1 SURVIVAL, HAZARD, AND CUMULATIVE HAZARD FUNCTIONS
Let T >= 0 denote a continuous random variable representing a subscriber's time until churn.
- Probability Density Function (PDF):
      f(t) = lim_{dt -> 0} P(t <= T < t + dt) / dt
- Survival Function:
      S(t) = P(T > t) = 1 - F(t) = \int_t^\infty f(u) du,   with S(0) = 1
- Instantaneous Hazard Rate:
      h(t) = lim_{dt -> 0} P(t <= T < t + dt | T >= t) / dt = f(t) / S(t) = -d/dt ln(S(t))
- Cumulative Hazard Function:
      H(t) = \int_0^t h(u) du = -ln(S(t))   ==>   S(t) = exp(-H(t))

2.2 PARAMETRIC WEIBULL DATA SYNTHESIS & RIGHT-CENSORING
For controlled experiments where true survival times are known, `SyntheticSurvivalDataGenerator` (`retainnet/data/generator.py`) samples event times from a proportional hazards Weibull model:
- Baseline Weibull hazard: h_0(t) = (k / lambda) * (t / lambda)^(k - 1), with shape k = 1.2 and scale lambda = 40.0 months.
- Covariate log-hazard shift: eta_i = (monthly_charges_i - 65) / 100 + 0.5 * I(contract_i == 0).
- Inverse-CDF sampling with uniform U_i ~ Uniform(1e-9, 1 - 1e-9):
      T_i* = lambda * (-ln(U_i))^(1 / k) / exp(eta_i)
- Administrative right-censoring time C_i ~ Uniform(1, 72).
- Observed duration T_i = max(1.0, min(T_i*, C_i)) and event indicator E_i = I(T_i* <= C_i).

2.3 STAGE 1: BEHAVIORAL SEGMENTATION VIA K-MEANS CLUSTERING
Implemented in `BehavioralSegmentationStage` (`retainnet/pipeline/stage1_segmentation.py`):
1. Numeric customer features are standardized using `StandardScaler` fitted on the training split.
2. `KMeans(n_clusters=k, random_state=seed, n_init=10)` partitions customers into k = 4 behavioral segments (if the training sample size N_train < k, k is automatically clamped to max(1, N_train)).
3. When `augment_features=True` is enabled on `RetainNetHybridPipeline`, one-hot cluster indicators in {0, 1}^k are concatenated to the feature matrix during both training (`fit_transform`) and inference (`transform_features`), keeping feature dimensions aligned across splits.

2.4 STAGE 2: TRAINING-SPLIT PROPENSITY BOUNDARY FILTERING
Implemented in `InstanceFilteringStage` (`retainnet/pipeline/stage2_filtering.py`):
1. A class-balanced L2-regularized logistic regression classifier (`LogisticRegression(class_weight='balanced', random_state=seed)`) is fitted on the training split using inverse-censoring sample weights.
2. For each training instance i, the model estimates the churn propensity score p_hat_i = P(E_i = 1 | x_i).
3. Training instances whose propensity falls inside the high-ambiguity band [low_threshold, high_threshold] (default [0.30, 0.70], or symmetric around 0.50 when `confidence_threshold` is supplied) are filtered out so Stage 3 models train on high-signal examples.
4. Safety fallback: If all training rows fall inside the ambiguity band, or if the training split has only a single class, the filter retains 100% of the training rows so downstream models never receive an empty array.
5. Evaluation isolation: Stage 2 filtering is applied only to the training set. Validation and held-out test sets are evaluated at 100% sample retention (0% filtered).

2.5 STAGE 3: DEEPSURV NEURAL PROPORTIONAL HAZARDS & BRESLOW TIE HANDLING
Implemented in `DeepSurvModel` (`retainnet/models/deep_surv.py`):
DeepSurv parameterizes the log-risk function g_theta(x) with a multilayer perceptron (`[64, 32, 16]` hidden units, `RobustBatchNorm1d`, `ReLU`, and `Dropout(0.2)`):
    h(t | x_i) = h_0(t) * exp(g_theta(x_i))
Predicted log-risk scores are clamped to [-20.0, 20.0] to prevent floating-point overflow in `exp()`.

When multiple individuals fail at the exact same observed time t_k, `DeepSurvModel._negative_log_likelihood` computes the exact Breslow (1974) tied partial log-likelihood in vectorized O(N) PyTorch operations:
1. Sort batch observations in descending order of observed time T_i.
2. Compute cumulative sums of exp(g_theta(x_j)) along the sorted tensor.
3. Group tied timestamps using `torch.unique_consecutive(T_sorted, return_inverse=True, return_counts=True)` and map every tied failure at time t_k to the complete risk set sum R(t_k) = {j : T_j >= t_k}.
4. Evaluate the negative average partial log-likelihood over observed events:
      L(theta) = - (1 / N_E) * \sum_{i : E_i = 1} [ g_theta(x_i) - ln( \sum_{j \in R(T_i)} exp(g_theta(x_j)) + 10^-8 ) ]
If a mini-batch contains zero events (N_E = 0), the loss returns a zero tensor connected to the computation graph (`risk_scores.sum() * 0.0`).

2.6 BRESLOW BASELINE HAZARD ESTIMATOR & SURVIVAL INTERPOLATION
After optimizing network weights theta, `DeepSurvModel._compute_baseline_hazard` computes the non-parametric Breslow cumulative baseline hazard H_0(t) over the training set:
    H_0(t) = \sum_{t_k <= t} [ d_k / \sum_{j \in R(t_k)} exp(g_theta(x_j)) ]
where d_k is the number of events at distinct failure time t_k. The baseline survival curve is S_0(t) = exp(-H_0(t)), and individual survival predictions are evaluated as:
    S(t | x_i) = [ S_0(t) ]^{ exp(g_theta(x_i)) }
If the training set contains zero observed events (`sum(E) == 0`), the estimator sets `baseline_survival = [1.0]` so `predict_survival_function` returns a flat survival curve S(t | x_i) = 1.0 without raising interpolation errors.

2.7 DEEPHIT DISCRETE-TIME PMF SURVIVAL NETWORK
Implemented in `DeepHitModel` (`retainnet/models/deep_hit.py`):
DeepHit partitions the time range [0, T_max] into K equal bins with bin edges [e_0, e_1, ..., e_K] and bin midpoints m_k = 0.5 * (e_{k-1} + e_k). The network outputs a softmax probability mass vector y_hat(x_i) = (y_{i,1}, ..., y_{i,K}) over the K bins.
- Training loss (Lee et al., 2018 discrete survival log-likelihood):
  Uncensored events (E_i = 1) in bin k_i maximize ln(y_{i, k_i}), while right-censored records (E_i = 0) in bin k_i maximize the log survival probability at the start of bin k_i: ln(1 - \sum_{m < k_i} y_{i,m}).
- Survival curve reconstruction:
  To avoid half-bin shift bias and right-edge collapse, `predict_survival_function` anchors survival at S(0) = 1.0, evaluates midpoint survival S(m_k | x_i) = 1 - (CDF_k - 0.5 * PMF_k) at each bin center m_k, and linearly interpolates across requested horizons.

2.8 DISCOUNTED DYNAMIC CLTV INTEGRATION VIA TRAPEZOIDAL QUADRATURE
Implemented in `DynamicLTVCalculator` (`retainnet/ltv/calculator.py`):
Given an N x M survival matrix `isd_matrix` evaluated at time grid `times = [t_1, ..., t_M]`, per-customer monthly revenue `arpu` (scalar or length-N array), monthly discount rate `d` (default 0.01), and acquisition cost `CAC` (default $120.00):
1. Discount factors are computed at each grid point: D(t_m) = (1 + d)^(-t_m).
2. Expected discounted monthly cash flows are formed by broadcasting:
      CF_{i, m} = S(t_m | x_i) * ARPU_i * D(t_m)
3. The continuous integral over [t_1, t_M] is approximated via vectorized trapezoidal quadrature (`np.trapezoid` / `np.trapz`) using the actual time step widths dt_m = t_{m+1} - t_m, and `CAC` is subtracted:
      CLTV_i = \sum_{m=1}^{M-1} 0.5 * (CF_{i,m} + CF_{i,m+1}) * (t_{m+1} - t_m) - CAC
Because the quadrature weights each interval by its calendar width dt_m, subdividing the same [1, 48] month horizon into 48 or 96 grid points yields consistent CLTV figures.

2.9 COUNTERFACTUAL RETENTION SCENARIO MODELING (WHAT-IF SIMULATOR)
In Tab 4 of `dashboard.py`, users can test hypothetical retention offers on a baseline survival profile:
- Monthly bill discount ($0 to $35/mo, clamped at $30 effective discount ceiling):
      discount_factor = max(0.65, 1.0 - 0.015 * min(discount, 30.0))
- 12-Month contract lock-in toggle: multiplier = 0.60 when active, 1.0 otherwise.
- VIP concierge onboarding toggle ($25 one-time cost): multiplier = 0.85 when active, 1.0 otherwise.
- Compound hazard ratio:
      gamma = discount_factor * (0.60 if lock_in else 1.0) * (0.85 if vip else 1.0)
- Counterfactual survival curve:
      S*(t | x) = [ S_base(t | x) ]^gamma
- Scenario LTV and ROI heuristic:
      area_ratio = (\sum_m S*(t_m)) / (\sum_m S_base(t_m))
      gross_counter_ltv = round(base_ltv * area_ratio)
      campaign_cost = 12.0 * discount + (25.0 if vip else 0.0)
      net_counter_ltv = gross_counter_ltv - campaign_cost
      net_lift = net_counter_ltv - base_ltv
      roi_pct = round((net_lift / campaign_cost) * 100) if campaign_cost > 0 else 0

2.10 CONFORMAL SURVIVAL CALIBRATION, DRIFT DETECTION, AND COLD-START PRIORS
1. `ConformalSurvivalCalibrator` (`retainnet/advanced/conformal_calibration.py`):
   Computes censoring-aware non-conformity scores on a calibration split at each subject's observed time T_i:
   - If right-censored (`E_i == 0`), the subject is confirmed active at T_i (`actual_indicator = 1.0`), so `score_i = |S(T_i | x_i) - 1.0|`.
   - If an event occurred (`E_i == 1`), failure occurred at T_i (`actual_indicator = 0.0`), so `score_i = |S(T_i | x_i) - 0.0|`.
   The finite-sample conformal quantile q at level 1 - alpha (default 90%, alpha = 0.10) is extracted at index ceil((1 - alpha)(N_cal + 1)) - 1, yielding prediction intervals `clip(S(t|x) +/- q, 0.0, 1.0)`.
2. `DriftDetector` (`retainnet/advanced/drift_detector.py`):
   Compares reference training feature distributions against evaluation cohorts using 10-bin Population Stability Index (PSI) and two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) statistics, flagging drift when PSI > 0.20 or KS p-value < 0.05.
3. `AmortizedSurvivalInference` (`retainnet/advanced/amortized_inference.py`):
   Computes empirical Kaplan-Meier baseline survival trajectories per K-Means cluster during training so new signups with no longitudinal history can be assigned a cluster prior survival curve immediately.

3.2 DATA & VALIDATION LAYER (retainnet/data/)
- `generator.py` (`SyntheticSurvivalDataGenerator`):
  Generates synthetic subscriber cohorts with configurable sample count `n_samples` and `seed`. Produces `customer_id`, `duration`, `event`, `monthly_charges`, `total_charges`, `contract_type`, `support_calls`, `num_services`, `paperless_billing`, and demographic attributes.
- `pipeline.py` (`DataPreprocessingPipeline`):
  Cleans currency strings (`$`, `€`, `£`, `₹`, commas), engineers domain ratios (`AvgMonthlyCharge`, `ChargesPerService`, `contract_risk`), imputes missing numeric values with training medians and missing categoricals with training modes, scales numeric columns via `MinMaxScaler`, and one-hot encodes categorical columns. Uses `drop_first=True` during `fit_transform` and aligns `transform` columns to `self.feature_names` so single-row (`N=1`) inference calls preserve non-reference categorical values accurately. Passing `drop_total_charges=True` removes cumulative charge columns to prevent tenure leakage.
- `telco_data.py` (`TelcoDatasetLoader`):
  Reads standard IBM Telco Customer Churn CSV files or generates a 2,500-subscriber benchmark dataset when no file path is provided. Also adapts non-Telco CSV files via `validate_customer_csv` when invoked from the CLI.
- `validator.py` (`validate_customer_csv`, `generate_csv_template`, `ValidationResult`):
  Validates uploaded CSV files up to 25 MB. Maps column aliases across Telco, Retail Sales (`daily_sales`, `units_sold`, `product_category`, `store_id`), E-Commerce, and SaaS schemas; strips currency symbols and thousand separators; validates non-negative numeric ranges and binary event indicators; deduplicates or disambiguates customer IDs; and neutralizes spreadsheet formula payloads.

3.3 3-STAGE PIPELINE LAYER (retainnet/pipeline/)
- `stage1_segmentation.py` (`BehavioralSegmentationStage`):
  Standardizes input features and fits K-Means clustering (`n_clusters=4`).
- `stage2_filtering.py` (`InstanceFilteringStage`):
  Fits a balanced logistic regression model with inverse-censoring weights and returns a boolean `keep_mask` excising ambiguous training records inside `[low_threshold, high_threshold]`.
- `hybrid_pipeline.py` (`RetainNetHybridPipeline`):
  Coordinates Stage 1 and Stage 2. `fit_transform(X, T, E)` returns `X_filtered`, `T_filtered`, `E_filtered`, `cluster_labels`, `cluster_labels_filtered`, `keep_mask`, `retention_rate`, and `retention_rate_pct`. `transform_features(X)` applies Stage 1 feature augmentation to validation/test matrices without dropping any rows.

3.4 SURVIVAL MODELING LAYER (retainnet/models/)
- `deep_surv.py` (`DeepSurvModel`, `DeepSurvNetwork`, `RobustBatchNorm1d`):
  PyTorch proportional hazards network with vectorized Breslow tied-event partial log-likelihood, gradient clipping (`max_norm=1.0`), Breslow baseline survival estimation, and deterministic seeding. `RobustBatchNorm1d` evaluates single-sample batches (`x.size(0) == 1`) using running statistics so `N=1` training or inference never raises a BatchNorm error.
- `deep_hit.py` (`DeepHitModel`):
  PyTorch discrete-time survival model outputting a softmax PMF across `num_bins` intervals. Trained with full uncensored PMF + right-censored survival log-likelihood, with S(0)=1.0 anchoring and bin-midpoint survival interpolation.
- `baselines.py` (`CoxPHBaselineModel`, `RandomSurvivalForestBaselineModel`):
  `CoxPHBaselineModel` wraps `lifelines.CoxPHFitter` with L2 penalization (`penalizer=0.01`) and collinearity fallbacks. `RandomSurvivalForestBaselineModel` wraps `sksurv.ensemble.RandomSurvivalForest` when installed, or sets `is_fallback=True` (`model_display_name="RSF (Fallback)"`) and uses an exponential constant-hazard baseline when `scikit-survival` is absent.

3.5 EVALUATION METRICS LAYER (retainnet/metrics/)
- `evaluation.py` (`Evaluator`):
  Computes Harrell's Concordance Index (`c_index`), Kaplan-Meier censoring distribution G(t) = P(C > t), Graf et al. (1999) Inverse Probability of Censoring Weighted (IPCW) Brier Score at horizon t (`brier_score_at_t`), trapezoidal Integrated Brier Score (`integrated_brier_score`), and Chi-square D-Calibration (`d_calibration`).

3.6 DYNAMIC CLTV LAYER (retainnet/ltv/)
- `calculator.py` (`DynamicLTVCalculator`, `sanitize_csv_cell`):
  Vectorized trapezoidal CLTV integrator supporting scalar or per-customer ARPU arrays, configurable monthly discount rate, and acquisition cost. `generate_ltv_report` builds a DataFrame with sanitized customer IDs, calculated LTV, 12-month and 24-month retention probabilities, and recommended retention actions.

3.7 COMMAND-LINE INTERFACE (main.py)
Supports `--dataset {synthetic,telco}`, `--telco-path`, `--model {coxph,rsf,deepsurv,deephit,all}`, `--n-samples`, `--n-clusters`, `--epochs`, `--horizon`, `--seed`, `--stage2-ablation`, and `--drop-total-charges`. Performs a stratified 60% train / 20% validation / 20% held-out test split, selects the best model using validation C-Index, evaluates all models on 100% of the held-out test set, prints the Dynamic CLTV summary, and optionally runs a side-by-side Stage 2 ablation comparison.

3.8 INTERACTIVE STREAMLIT DASHBOARD (dashboard.py)
Trains the survival pipeline on the 2,500-record IBM Telco Benchmark cohort (cached via `@st.cache_resource`), evaluates 100% of the 500-record held-out test split, and serializes the trained PyTorch `DeepSurv` MLP weights, `CoxPH` coefficients, K-Means centroids, `StandardScaler` parameters, and `AmortizedSurvivalInference` cluster priors into the client-side bundle for interactive exploration across 6 tabs.

4. EXPERIMENTAL EVALUATION & BENCHMARKS
====================================================================================================

4.1 HELD-OUT TEST SET EVALUATION PROTOCOL
To prevent optimistic bias:
1. Feature scalers (`MinMaxScaler` / `StandardScaler`), K-Means centroids, and the Stage 2 logistic filter are fitted strictly on the training split.
2. Stage 2 propensity filtering is applied only to the training split to remove ambiguous boundary examples during gradient descent.
3. Model selection in `main.py` chooses the top architecture based on validation-set C-Index.
4. Final metrics are reported on 100% of the held-out test split (0% filtered).

4.2 CONCORDANCE INDEX (HARRELL'S C-INDEX) & IPCW BRIER SCORES
- Harrell's C-Index measures the proportion of permissible subscriber pairs (i, j) where the customer who churned earlier (T_i < T_j, E_i = 1) was assigned a higher predicted risk score (or lower expected survival probability). A value of 0.50 indicates random ordering and 1.00 indicates ideal concordance.
- IPCW Brier Score at horizon t weights squared survival prediction errors by the inverse probability of censoring G(t) estimated via Kaplan-Meier on (T_i, 1 - E_i), correcting for right-censored observations prior to time t.
- Integrated Brier Score (IBS) integrates BS(t) over the full forecast horizon and divides by the time span (t_M - t_1).

4.3 STAGE 2 ABLATION STUDY: FILTERED VS. UNFILTERED TRAINING
Running `python main.py --dataset synthetic --model all --epochs 30 --n-samples 500 --stage2-ablation` trains each selected architecture twice—once on the Stage 2 denoised training subset and once on 100% of the unfiltered training split—and evaluates both on the same held-out test set. This allows direct inspection of the sample-size vs. boundary-denoising tradeoff on any dataset.

4.4 COLLINEARITY BETWEEN TotalCharges AND TENURE (--drop-total-charges)
In raw telecom billing exports (`WA_Fn-UseC_-Telco-Customer-Churn.csv`), `TotalCharges` is the cumulative sum of monthly bills over the customer's tenure: `TotalCharges ≈ MonthlyCharges * tenure`. Because `tenure` is the survival duration target T, keeping both `TotalCharges` and `MonthlyCharges` allows a model to approximate T via `TotalCharges / MonthlyCharges`. Passing `--drop-total-charges` to `main.py` (or `drop_total_charges=True` to `DataPreprocessingPipeline`) removes `TotalCharges` and `AvgMonthlyCharge` before training.

6. VIVA & PROJECT PRESENTATION Q&A
====================================================================================================

Q1: Why use survival analysis instead of logistic regression or XGBoost for customer churn?
A1: Binary classifiers predict whether a customer will churn within a fixed window, which forces us to either ignore right-censored active subscribers or mislabel short-tenure active subscribers as permanent non-churners. Survival analysis models the continuous time-to-event distribution P(T > t | x), incorporates right-censored records accurately up to their last observed month via risk sets, and produces a full retention trajectory over time that can be integrated directly into discounted cash-flow CLTV.

Q2: Why does Stage 2 filter only the training split and never the validation or test splits?
A2: Stage 2 removes ambiguous boundary instances (0.30 <= p_hat <= 0.70) so the neural survival network learns cleaner hazard gradients during training. In production deployment, however, a model must score every incoming subscriber—including ambiguous ones. Keeping 100% of validation and held-out test records unfiltered guarantees that reported C-Index and Brier Scores reflect real-world generalization performance.

Q3: How does DeepSurv handle tied event times during backpropagation?
A3: When multiple customers churn at the exact same tenure t_k, standard sequential ordering can introduce tie-breaking bias. `DeepSurvModel._negative_log_likelihood` uses Breslow's partial likelihood formulation via `torch.unique_consecutive`, assigning all tied events at t_k the same complete risk-set denominator \sum_{j : T_j >= t_k} exp(g_theta(x_j)) in vectorized O(N) tensor operations.

Q4: How is continuous CLTV calculated from a discrete grid of survival probabilities?
A4: `DynamicLTVCalculator.calculate_ltv` multiplies each customer's survival probability S(t_m | x_i) by their monthly revenue ARPU_i and the discount factor (1 + d)^(-t_m) at each grid point t_m, integrates the resulting cash-flow curve over time using the trapezoidal rule weighted by dt_m = t_{m+1} - t_m, and subtracts the acquisition cost CAC.

Q5: Why does TotalCharges cause target leakage in raw Telco data, and how is it handled?
A5: In raw Telco billing tables, `TotalCharges` is roughly `MonthlyCharges * tenure`. Since `tenure` is used as the survival duration T, a model given both `TotalCharges` and `MonthlyCharges` can reconstruct T directly. Passing `--drop-total-charges` removes `TotalCharges` and `AvgMonthlyCharge` from the preprocessing pipeline so the survival models rely solely on legitimate behavioral and contractual features.

Q6: How does the pipeline behave when a batch has only 1 row or zero churn events?
A6: For single-row (`N=1`) inputs, `DeepSurvModel` and `DeepHitModel` use `RobustBatchNorm1d`, which switches to running statistics when `batch_size == 1`, and `DataPreprocessingPipeline.transform` preserves fitted dummy columns without dropping the active category. When a training dataset has zero churn events (`sum(E) == 0`), all survival models fall back cleanly to a flat survival curve S(t) = 1.0 across all horizons.

Q7: What is the difference between DynamicLTVCalculator and the What-If Simulator?
A7: `DynamicLTVCalculator` computes the baseline econometric present value of a subscriber's cash flows using trapezoidal quadrature over their predicted survival curve. The What-If Simulator in Tab 4 applies a proportional hazard dampener gamma (S*(t) = S(t)^gamma) to estimate how promotional discounts, annual contract lock-ins, or concierge onboarding could shift retention and net campaign ROI.

Q8: How are uploaded CSV files sanitized against spreadsheet formula injection?
A8: Any untrusted string or customer identifier starting with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, `\t`, or `\r` could be executed as a formula or macro when opened in Excel or LibreOffice Calc. `sanitize_csv_cell` in `retainnet/ltv/calculator.py` (and `sanitizeCSVCellJS` in `dashboard.py`) prefixes leading formula characters with a single quote (`'`) on both CSV ingestion and CSV export.
